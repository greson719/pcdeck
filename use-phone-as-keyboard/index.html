How to Use an Android Phone as a Keyboard for Windows

When a laptop keyboard spills out, physical keys jam, or you need a dedicated numeric keypad for data entry, your Android phone can transmit keystrokes directly into Windows over local Wi-Fi.

Updated 27 August 2026 · Windows 10 & 11 · Includes Numpad & Modifier Keys · Offline LAN

Direct Local Keyboard

PCDeck sends raw key codes directly over local Wi-Fi to Windows. It supports regular typing, Android voice dictation, modifier chords (Ctrl+Alt+Del), and a full numeric keypad.

Why turn your phone into a PC keyboard?

Having a phone-based keyboard available is essential in several common situations:

  • Hardware keyboard failure: Liquid spills or broken membrane switches leave you unable to type your Windows login PIN or password.
  • Laptops without numeric keypads: Modern ultrabooks omit the 10-key numpad. PCDeck gives you a dedicated numpad for Excel spreadsheets, accounting software, and blender hotkeys.
  • Media center and couch PC control: Type search queries into Netflix, YouTube, or browser address bars on a TV-connected PC without carrying a bulky physical keyboard.
  • Voice-to-text dictation: Use your phone's advanced voice recognition (Gboard / Samsung Voice) to dictate long paragraphs directly into Word, Notepad, or your browser on Windows.

Step-by-step setup

  1. Launch PCDeck on your Windows PC

    Run PCDeck.exe. The status panel confirms the local input server is active on your local network port (default 5000/5001).

  2. Connect your phone to the same network

    Ensure your Android device is on the same local Wi-Fi or connected via phone mobile hotspot.

  3. Open PCDeck on Android and switch to Keyboard

    Open the app, scan the QR code to pair, and tap the Keyboard tab in the navigation bar.

  4. Type or dictate into any Windows program

    Place your PC cursor in any text field (Word, browser, command prompt). Keystrokes typed on the phone appear instantaneously on the PC screen.

Using phone voice dictation on Windows

Windows voice typing can be clunky or require online speech packs. With PCDeck, you can leverage your Android keyboard's built-in microphone button (e.g. Google Voice Typing). When you speak into your phone, the speech-to-text engine converts your voice to text and PCDeck sends the resulting string directly to your active Windows document.
