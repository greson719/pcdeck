Understanding Decibels (dBFS) and Microphone Input Levels

In digital audio, volume is measured in Decibels relative to Full Scale (dBFS). Because digital maximum is 0 dBFS, all input signals are represented as negative numbers.

Volume Range Digital Level Voice Quality Assessment Recommended Action
Clipping Distortion 0 dBFS to -3 dBFS Harsh robotic distortion; audio peaks square off. Lower your Windows microphone gain in Sound Settings.
Ideal Speech Level -6 dBFS to -18 dBFS Crystal clear speech; optimal signal-to-noise ratio. Perfect for Discord, Zoom, Teams, and voice streaming.
Quiet Speech -20 dBFS to -35 dBFS Muffled or faint; listeners will struggle to hear you. Move closer to mic or enable Windows Microphone Boost.
Noise Floor / Room Ambience -45 dBFS to -60 dBFS Background noise (AC hum, PC fans, keyboard typing). Normal ambient room level when quiet.

How to Fix Microphone Issues in Windows 10 & 11

  1. Check Windows Input Volume: Open Windows Run (Win + R), type mmsys.cpl, press Enter. Go to the Recording tab, double-click your active microphone, open the Levels tab, and set volume to between 75% and 85%.
  2. Disable Audio Enhancements if Robotic: In the microphone properties window, navigate to the Advanced or Enhancements tab and check Disable all enhancements to bypass faulty manufacturer DSP filters.
  3. Use PCDeck When Headset Fails: If your PC headset wire is frayed or your laptop mic captures too much keyboard clicking, PCDeck streams audio directly from your smartphone's noise-cancelling dual-microphone array to your Windows PC over local Wi-Fi.

Frequently Asked Questions

How does the "Hear Yourself" echo test work?

When you click Record Echo Sample, your browser buffers 5 seconds of raw audio and renders an instant audio player so you can hear exactly how your voice sounds to participants in online meetings.

Why is my microphone volume showing red (clipping)?

Clipping occurs when the electrical signal from your microphone exceeds the analog-to-digital converter threshold. Lower your Windows input gain or back away slightly from the microphone capsule.

Can I test wireless Bluetooth and USB microphones?

Yes. Select your Bluetooth headset, USB condenser mic, or virtual PCDeck audio input from the device selector menu above.
