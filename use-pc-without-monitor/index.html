How to use a PC without a monitor

Your laptop screen shattered, your external monitor died, or you need to control a headless PC tucked away in a closet. Here is how to diagnose the issue blindly and use your phone as an emergency display.

Updated 26 August 2026 · Windows 10 and 11 · Emergency troubleshooting

Emergency display

If you need to see and control your desktop immediately without spare cables or monitors, PCDeck streams your full Windows screen to an Android phone over local Wi-Fi with touch-screen controls. Jump to the phone screen setup or continue below for blind hardware shortcuts.

First: Quick Blind Troubleshooting

Before assuming total hardware loss, Windows provides blind keyboard shortcuts to toggle video outputs and audio accessibility:

Blind shortcuts for monitor issues
ShortcutAction
Win + PCycles projection modes (PC Screen Only, Duplicate, Extend, Second Screen Only). Press Win+P, then ↓ then Enter to switch modes if a TV or secondary cable is attached.
Win + Ctrl + Shift + BRestarts the Windows Graphics Driver. You will hear a short beep; this clears frozen black-screen driver glitches.
Win + Ctrl + EnterTurns on Windows Narrator. Speaks every focused element through your speakers so you can navigate blind.
Alt + F4, then EnterSafe graceful shutdown from desktop.

Connecting to an Emergency Screen (TV / HDMI)

If your laptop display is black, plug an HDMI or USB-C cable into a television or living room monitor. Press Win + P, press ↓ twice, then press Enter to force Windows to Duplicate the display.

Use Your Android Phone as an Emergency Screen with PCDeck

If you don't have a spare monitor or TV, PCDeck streams your entire computer screen to your phone over Wi-Fi so you can see what's happening and tap icons directly.

  1. Start your PC

    Turn on your computer. PCDeck opens automatically in the background even if no screen is plugged in.

  2. Open PCDeck on your phone

    Connect your phone to the same Wi-Fi (or your phone's hotspot) and open the app.

  3. See your desktop and tap to click

    Your Windows desktop appears live on your phone screen. You can tap buttons, open apps, and type using the on-screen keyboard.
