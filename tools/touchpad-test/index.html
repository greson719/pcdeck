Understanding Touchpad Polling Rates (Hz) and Input Latency (ms)

Input latency is the physical delay between the instant your finger moves on a touchpad and the moment your operating system registers that cursor displacement. It is directly governed by two factors: the polling rate (Hz) of the input sensor and the DPC / kernel dispatch latency of your operating system.

Why Do Some Laptop Touchpads Feel Sluggish or "Floaty"?

Users often describe poor touchpad responsiveness as a "floaty" or "ice-skating" sensation. This is rarely caused by browser rendering. The most common underlying causes include:

  • Windows Pointer Ballistics & Precision Acceleration: Windows applies non-linear acceleration curves to mouse and touchpad inputs by default. While helpful for small trackpads, it adds artificial velocity delays.
  • I2C / HID Bus Throttling: Many laptop manufacturers cap internal I2C bus interrupt rates to 100 Hz or 125 Hz while running on battery power to prevent CPU package C-state wakeups.
  • Bluetooth Polling Rate Ceiling: Standard Bluetooth wireless mice and touchpads are limited by the Bluetooth Human Interface Device (HID) specification to between 90 Hz and 133 Hz. High-speed local Wi-Fi or 2.4GHz proprietary dongles deliver substantially higher throughput.
  • Display Refresh Synchronization: If your monitor operates at 60 Hz, moving a 125 Hz cursor can create micro-stutter due to mismatched frame delivery times. High-refresh 120Hz/144Hz displays dramatically improve visual tracking continuity.

How to Optimize Laptop Touchpad Latency on Windows 10 & 11

  1. Enable Windows Precision Touchpad High Sensitivity: Navigate to Settings > Bluetooth & devices > Touchpad. Set Cursor speed to medium-high and verify that Tap sensitivity is set to Most sensitive.
  2. Disable "Enhance Pointer Precision": Open Windows Run (Win + R), type main.cpl, press Enter. Go to the Pointer Options tab and uncheck Enhance pointer precision to enable true linear 1:1 hardware tracking.
  3. Connect to AC Power: Modern Intel and AMD laptops dynamically throttle internal sensor polling when unplugged. Testing while connected to wall power reveals true hardware capability.
  4. Use PCDeck for Emergency Zero-Lag Input: If your physical laptop touchpad is physically damaged, sticky, or limited to 60 Hz, launching PCDeck lets your smartphone screen act as an instant high-frequency multi-touch surface over local Wi-Fi.

Frequently Asked Questions

How does this online test measure touchpad polling rate?

This benchmark registers high-frequency pointer listeners using the standard W3C Pointer Events API. When supported by your browser, it queries event.getCoalescedEvents() to extract intermediate sub-frame sensor updates. The elapsed time between consecutive hardware reports is measured via performance.now() with sub-millisecond precision.

What is considered a "good" touchpad polling rate?

For standard laptop trackpads, a stable 120 Hz to 150 Hz with jitter under 3.0 ms is considered excellent. External gaming mice typically achieve 500 Hz or 1000 Hz. Anything below 60 Hz will feel visibly choppy.

Can I test my smartphone or tablet screen with this tool?

Yes. Open this page in Safari or Chrome on your smartphone or tablet and drag your finger across the canvas. Most modern mobile displays report touch input at 120 Hz to 240 Hz.

Why is jitter stability important?

A consistent 125 Hz polling rate (where updates arrive evenly every 8.0 ms) feels significantly smoother than a 250 Hz sensor that experiences large timing spikes. High jitter causes sudden cursor jumps or skipped frames.
